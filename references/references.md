## Survey and Foundational Papers

### 1. Undiscovered Public Knowledge

**Swanson, D. R. (1986).** *Undiscovered Public Knowledge.* The Library Quarterly, 56(2), 103–118.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/601720)

Foundational work on discovering previously unconnected knowledge, providing an important conceptual basis for literature-based hypothesis generation.

### 2. Scientific Discovery: Computational Explorations of the Creative Processes

**Langley, P., Simon, H. A., Bradshaw, G. L., & Zytkow, J. M. (1987).** *Scientific Discovery: Computational Explorations of the Creative Processes.* MIT Press.

[ISBN / Book Information](https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/4759/Scientific-DiscoveryComputational-Explorations-of)

A foundational computational treatment of scientific discovery and automated hypothesis formation.

## Hypothesis Generation and Research-Idea Generation

### 3. Hypothesis Generation with Large Language Models

**Zhou, Y., Liu, H., Srivastava, T., Mei, H., & Tan, C. (2024).** *Hypothesis Generation with Large Language Models.* Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on NLP for Science (NLP4Science), 117–139.

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.nlp4science-1.10)

Examines the use of large language models for generating scientific hypotheses.

### 4. Large Language Models for Automated Open-domain Scientific Hypotheses Discovery

**Yang, Z., Du, X., Li, J., Zheng, J., Poria, S., & Cambria, E. (2024).** *Large Language Models for Automated Open-domain Scientific Hypotheses Discovery.* Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024, 13545–13565.

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.804)

Investigates automated open-domain scientific hypothesis discovery using large language models.

### 5. Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers

**Si, C., Yang, D., & Hashimoto, T. (2025).** *Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers.* International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2025).

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)

Studies the ability of LLMs to generate novel research ideas and compares them with ideas produced by NLP researchers.

### 6. Scientific Hypothesis Generation by Large Language Models: Laboratory Validation in Breast Cancer Treatment

**(2025).** *Scientific Hypothesis Generation by Large Language Models: Laboratory Validation in Breast Cancer Treatment.* Journal of the Royal Society Interface.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2024.0674) | [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12134935/)

Connects LLM-generated scientific hypotheses with laboratory validation in a biomedical research setting.

## Self-Improvement, Reflection, and Debate

### 7. Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate

**Du, Y., Li, S., Torralba, A., Tenenbaum, J. B., & Mordatch, I. (2024).** *Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate.* Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 235, 11733–11763.

[Paper / PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/du24e.html)

Investigates whether multiple language-model agents debating their answers can improve factuality and reasoning.

### 8. Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback

**Madaan, A., Tandon, N., Gupta, P., Hallinan, S., Gao, L., Wiegreffe, S., Alon, U., Dziri, N., Prabhumoye, S., Yang, Y., Gupta, S., Majumder, B. P., Hermann, K., Welleck, S., Yazdanbakhsh, A., & Clark, P. (2023).** *Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback.* Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 36.

[Paper / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)

Introduces an iterative approach in which an LLM generates, critiques, and refines its own output.

### 9. Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning

**Shinn, N., Cassano, F., Berman, E., Gopinath, A., Narasimhan, K., & Yao, S. (2023).** *Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning.* Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 36.

[Paper / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)

Introduces verbal reflection and memory mechanisms for improving the performance of language agents.

### 10. Should We Be Going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs

**Smit, A. P., Grinsztajn, N., Duckworth, P., Barrett, T. D., & Pretorius, A. (2024).** *Should We Be Going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs.* Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 235, 45883–45905.

[Paper / PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)

Examines different multi-agent debate strategies and their effectiveness and computational trade-offs.

### 11. CONSENSAGENT: Towards Efficient and Effective Consensus in Multi-Agent LLM Interactions Through Sycophancy Mitigation

**Pitre, P., Ramakrishnan, N., & Wang, X. (2025).** *CONSENSAGENT: Towards Efficient and Effective Consensus in Multi-Agent LLM Interactions Through Sycophancy Mitigation.* Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025, 22112–22133.

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-acl.1141)

Investigates consensus formation in multi-agent LLM interactions and methods for mitigating sycophancy.

R. D., Penadés, J. R., Peltz, G., Xu, Y., Pawlosky, A., Karthikesalingam, A., & Natarajan, V. (2025).** *Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist*

### 16. The Virtual Lab: AI Agents Design New SARS-CoV-2 Nanobodies with Experimental Validation
